5+ YEARS IN AAA DEVELOPMENT

Quality Design through a Player Experience lens.

My background is in Embedded Quality, but testing is now one part of a broader discipline. I work across technical quality, Player Experience, evidence, Live operations and player-facing teams to understand whether the experience being built is the experience players actually receive.

Quality Design applies quality expertise within development, using player perspective, evidence, technical investigation and risk analysis to influence how products are designed, built and improved.

My practice concentrates on the boundary between technical quality and player reality: connecting intended outcomes with evidence of what players actually experience, then translating that learning back into actionable questions for Quality, Design, Engineering and player-facing disciplines.

Evidence & Insight

I combine different forms of player evidence to interrogate assumptions. Research, BI, Community, Customer Support, telemetry, engagement information and direct observation answer different questions.

The aim is not to replace specialist researchers or data scientists, but to connect their evidence with testing, technical context and player behaviour so development teams can act on it.

That also includes building dashboards and evidence summaries that make trends, risks and unanswered questions easier to inspect rather than relying on individual anecdotes.

Evidence Synthesis ADO Dashboards Telemetry Player Sentiment Analysis Research / Insights Survey Analysis Technical Translation
 
 

Community Engineering

 

In a large live game, developers cannot treat millions of players like a direct customer conversation. Player evidence is distributed across Community, Customer Support, Player Experience, moderation, Research, telemetry and Live operations.

 

I work across that boundary. Player-facing teams often receive the earliest or messiest signals while technical teams have the context needed to investigate them. Community Engineering, for me, is the work of connecting those perspectives: understanding player behaviour and sentiment, translating technical complexity, and helping useful information travel in both directions.

 

That includes player-facing communication such as patch notes, where technical changes have to be turned into information that is accurate, understandable and useful to the people playing the game.

Technical & Adversarial Quality

I deliberately investigate how systems behave when players optimise, misuse, exploit or interact with them beyond the expected happy path.

This combines conventional Quality skills with player psychology and domain knowledge. The question is not only "does this feature work?" but "what happens when thousands or millions of motivated players start looking for the limits of the system?"

Technical Risk Analysis Destructive Testing Exploratory Testing Regression Testing Cross-Play Testing Root Cause Analysis Log Analysis Exploits & Anti-Cheat Bad-Faith Behaviour
 
 

Product & Production Practice

 

Some of my work overlaps with Product and Production without implying ownership of an entire roadmap.

 

I help frame player problems, establish useful questions, combine evidence, identify risk, facilitate between disciplines and keep attention on outcomes after work reaches Live. On the Production side, I contribute to triage, readiness, dependencies, operational follow-through and making ambiguous problems easier for teams to act on.

 

I think of this as collaborative product work: creating enough clarity for the people who own Design, Engineering, Production, Research, Quality and player-facing disciplines to make better decisions together.

SMALL-SCALE LIVE GAME OPERATIONS

Persistent worlds at human scale.

I use "Micro-Live Service" to describe my work designing and operating smaller persistent roleplay game environments.

These are not one-shot game sessions. Players inhabit the world continuously, roleplay with one another, create their own stories and social structures, and continue producing content and interaction outside the game between play sessions.

The audience is far smaller than a commercial AAA live service, but many of the underlying problems are recognisable: retention, onboarding, player behaviour, balance, communication, moderation, feedback, content cadence and understanding how a change affects a living community.

At this scale there are fewer specialist departments, so I work across several disciplines directly. This lets game design, product management, community engineering, player feedback and Live administration reinforce one another rather than existing as separate functions.

Game Design

I design systems around actual player interaction rather than only intended behaviour. Mechanics have to survive contact with players who collaborate, compete, optimise, roleplay and create uses that were never explicitly designed for them.

Game Design Systems Design Player Behaviour Balance Emergent Play Roleplay Systems

Product Management

I decide which problems are worth solving, gather feedback, weigh competing player needs and decide how the environment should evolve.

At this scale, Product Management is direct and practical: identifying a player or community problem, deciding what outcome is wanted, choosing what to change, communicating it and then watching what happens after release.

Product Management Problem Framing Prioritisation Player Outcomes Feedback Loops Change Management

Community Engineering

Roleplay communities are part of the game system. Rules, communication, moderation practices and social structures influence what kinds of behaviour the environment encourages or suppresses.

I treat that as something to design rather than something that only happens after players arrive.

Community Engineering Community Operations Moderation Bad-Faith Behaviour Player Psychology Social Systems Communication

Data & Player Feedback

I use surveys and structured player feedback to understand how changes are being received and where the experience is not matching the intended outcome.

The smaller population makes qualitative context especially useful: numbers can identify a pattern, while player responses help explain why that pattern exists.

Survey Design Survey Analysis Player Sentiment Qualitative Analysis Feedback Synthesis Evidence-Led Iteration

Live Administration

Operating a persistent environment also means dealing with the unglamorous part of a live service: incidents, disputes, configuration, communication, policy, permissions and keeping the environment functioning while players are actively using it.
